[Download Wisp 0.5.0-rc1 (Apple silicon)](RELEASE_ZIP_URL)

**Wisp 0.5.0-rc1** is a release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it now comes inside the app. This candidate is mostly about the first ten minutes: setting Wisp up and getting a first useful answer.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself (for example Ollama or LM Studio, connected in Wisp's setup).
- 16 GB of memory at least. We tuned Wisp's engine settings for a 24 GB Mac and recommend that size; on 16 GB, close other heavy apps if replies stall. Wisp offers the model download only on Macs with 16 GB or more.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. Wisp doesn't ask for permissions by itself. If Brief or Day says a source isn't allowed, right-click the menu-bar icon, choose **Permissions…**, and click **Allow**. If Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

### A first run that sets you up

- **One setup window on first launch.** Five steps: Model (picks Gemma 4 E4B and downloads it), Permissions, Google, About you, and How to use. Every step is optional and can be finished later from the menu-bar icon (**Finish Setup…**). **Permissions…** reopens that step at any time.
- **Clear download feedback.** Wisp tells you before you start if you are short of disk space, shows progress, says "No progress, check your connection" if bytes stop moving, and shows whether a stopped download was paused or failed. You can keep going while it downloads, and Wisp sends a notification when it is ready, if you closed or minimized the setup window (and Notifications is allowed). When the model has downloaded, Wisp checks that it really answers.
- **Quit and come back.** Setup is finished only when you click **Done**. If you quit or close the window part-way, or macOS quits and reopens Wisp after you allow Full Disk Access, setup reopens on the next launch at the step you left. Pressing Return on the first step downloads.
- **Uses the oMLX you already have.** If oMLX is on your Mac, Wisp starts it once by itself, shows "Connecting to your oMLX…" until it answers, and switches to a Wisp model it already serves, so nothing is downloaded twice. The step offers **Try again** if oMLX doesn't answer within about 10 seconds. Wisp never looks through your folders for models.
- **Wisp never asks for a permission by itself.** Each one (Calendar, Reminders, Mail and Notes, Messages, Full Disk Access, Contacts, Notifications) is asked only when you click **Allow** on its row, with the reason shown. Contacts is offered once Wisp can read your Messages. Screen Recording and Accessibility are under **Grant More Access…**; without Screen Recording, Wisp skips reading other windows' text. **Grant More Access…** lists only what is still off.
- **One window per Dock click.** Every Wisp window can be minimized. A Dock click brings forward setup while it's unfinished, otherwise the window you used last.
- **Gemma 4 E4B is the default model**, about 5.5 GB. The engine is bundled, so there is nothing else to install. Wisp starts the engine's memory guard on "aggressive" (a stock oMLX starts on "balanced"), so the model loads on a 24 GB Mac with other apps open. A "balanced" setting is moved to "aggressive" once; any other tier you chose yourself is kept.
- **Getting to know you** is the setup window's About you step. Answer or skip it there and the notch won't offer it again; **Tell Wisp About You…** and `/about` reopen it.
- **VoiceOver labels and announcements** in the setup window, and the window fits small displays.
- **Wisp on Macs without a notch.** No fake notch is drawn any more. The menu-bar icon shows Wisp's status, and pushing the pointer against the top-centre edge opens the panel.
- **Quit really quits.** Quitting unloads the models and stops the engine, so your memory is free again.

### Brief and Day

- **Before you ask.** Brief opens with up to five cards for things Wisp already looked into across your mail, texts, notes, calendar and reminders, each with one button to finish it: draft a reply for you to edit, set a reminder, add an event, hold time to work, or open the original. Security and account alerts come first. Tap x on any card or Needs you row and it is gone; reminders also get a check to mark them done.
- **Day** places timed to-dos on a timeline and lists the rest under "Still to fit".
- **It notices when you handled something yourself** and clears the matching card within about a minute.
- **Add-to-calendar is off by default.** Wisp no longer adds events and reminders for you from old mail and texts; the same items arrive as cards you can tap to add.

**On your first day**

- **Brief and Day never say "all clear" about something Wisp couldn't read.** If Calendar, Reminders, Mail, Messages or Notes can't be read, the row says why in plain words and links to the fix. For Calendar, Reminders, or Mail and Notes that were never allowed, the link reads **Allow in Wisp…** and opens Wisp's own Permissions step. Otherwise it opens the System Settings page: Calendars, Reminders, Full Disk Access, Automation or Internet Accounts. For the first couple of minutes after launch you see "Reading your Calendar..." instead, because a real first read takes a few seconds.
- **Google mail that wasn't checked is named.** If a connected Google account's mail hasn't been read lately, Brief names the account and says either "Wisp hasn't finished checking" it (a check that is still running or running late) or "Couldn't check" it, with a prompt to reconnect in **Settings > Google** (an expired or incomplete sign-in). It shows the mail Wisp did read under that line, never as if it were all of it. If Mail on your Mac has that same account and read it, that read counts.
- **The day-one Daily Summary waits for your permission answers.** For the first 15 minutes after the very first launch, Wisp holds the first Daily Summary while Calendar, Reminders or Messages (Full Disk Access) are still waiting for you to answer, so it doesn't write "Calendar couldn't be read" about access you are about to give. It then rebuilds that first summary once, when a missing source arrives or when you finish Getting to know you.
- **Routine actions are offered first.** Wisp learns your routines on this Mac (switch it off with **Learn my routines** in the notch's quick settings). On a new install, what it learns reaches you as tap-to-add suggestions, and nothing is added for you for the first 3 days, or sooner once you tap Useful or Undo on any of them. After that, a routine reminder or a "Tentative:" calendar hold can be added for you, and Wisp tells you with an Undo.
- **A reply card opens the draft before it sends.** The first tap on a reply card shows the recipient and the exact words; **Send** is inside that draft. Wisp's service also refuses to send an email or text reply unless the request carries the words you were shown.

### Ask

- **Ask answers questions about Wisp itself.** "What can you do?", "Who are you?", "Is my data private?" and "How do I use Wisp?" get written answers that Wisp checks against your Mac right now, without waiting for the model, so they work on a first launch while the model is still loading.
- **The "what can you do" welcome only describes what is live.** It lists the apps Wisp can read right now (connected, still reading for the first time, or not connected and why), whether a Google account is connected, three try-it questions that fit those sources, and a feature only if this build has the tool for it.
- **Starters stay reachable.** After you use some of Ask's suggested questions, a **Try another** row offers the ones you haven't asked in this chat.
- **The privacy answer says where answers really come from.** On the default setup it says your data stays on this Mac. If you have turned on an optional cloud model, it says which questions go there and what is sent.
- **Getting to know you stays on this Mac.** What you tell Wisp (your name, goals, how you like replies) is kept in one file, `~/.moe/profile.json`, and used by Wisp's own on-Mac model. It is never sent to an optional cloud model (the Super Model, or a cloud model you gave Reasoning, Coding or Research). It travels only if you have turned all of Wisp onto a cloud model (the "Everything" setting, which can no longer be switched on in Settings), or to your own Mac on your tailnet that you set up and Wisp has verified. The panel's lock line says which of these applies, and when Wisp can't read the cloud settings it assumes the answers stay here.
- **Erase deletes it.** **Erase everything** in the Wisp window's Memory > About you removes every answer, including any unreadable copy Wisp had set aside, and Wisp won't offer the questions again by itself.

### Setting up from the notch

- **The model download is in the notch's setup card.** When Wisp can't answer yet and the setup window is closed, the card shows the recommended model, a **Download** button, and then a progress bar with the amount downloaded, the speed and the time left. It says "No progress, check your connection" if the bytes stop moving, "Download paused" with **Resume** if it was paused, and "Download didn't finish" with **Try again** if it failed. You can close the panel; the download keeps going.
- **Your Mac stays awake while bytes are moving.** Wisp stops your Mac from falling asleep on its own during the download. It lets go if nothing has moved for 10 minutes, if progress can't be read for 90 seconds in a row, or after 6 hours. Closing the lid still puts the Mac to sleep, and if that interrupts the download, **Resume** picks up where it left off.
- **Wisp switches to the model when it finishes**, even if it had stopped holding your Mac awake by then. The notch card's **Use & test** step confirms the model answers.
- **No download is offered where it can't run.** On a Mac with less than 16 GB of memory, Wisp doesn't offer a model its engine would refuse to load. It says so and points to connecting a smaller model you already run in another app.

### Google (for testers on the list)

- **Connect Google** from **Settings > Google** (**Connect Google account**), or from the notch's Google row (**Connect Google**) before you have connected an account. Wisp reads your Gmail and Google Calendar, on your Mac, to build Brief and Day.
- **You must be on the tester list.** Ask the developer to add the Google address you will use. Others see "Access blocked".
- **Google shows an "unverified app" warning.** Choose **Continue**.
- **One connection covers everything, read-only.** The sign-in asks, in one go, to read your Gmail (required), your Calendar, and your Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Chat, Tasks and Contacts. All of it is read-only, and you can untick anything except Gmail on Google's screen. Wisp can't send, edit or delete anything in Google with that connection. If you untick some, the account still connects and Settings offers **Reconnect** to add them later. Asking about those files uses Google tools that Wisp sets up the first time you connect (a small download, so connect while online), and Ask asks first before a Google tool that sends, creates, updates, deletes or shares something.
- **Sign-in expires every 7 days** while Wisp is in testing (Google's rule for apps in testing, not Wisp's). Wisp sends one notification and Brief names the account; reconnect from **Settings > Google**.
- **Disconnect & erase** revokes Wisp's access and removes that account's mail, events and to-dos from Wisp.

### Safety

- Wisp does not send an email or message, or change your calendar from a card, until you approve it.
- Full access no longer means "never ask". Under full access, Wisp still asks first before it runs AppleScript, or a shell command that could send something off your Mac, drive another app or read a key or saved sign-in. It always asks before opening Shortcuts, Terminal, a link that isn't a web page (like mailto: or shortcuts:), or a file that runs something when opened. After Wisp has read a file, web page, mail or message in the same turn, it also asks before it opens a web link, fetches a web page, runs a Shortcut, places a call, sends a web request, or uses an action from a connected server or skill. The card shows the exact command, the full text of the AppleScript, or the script a command runs, so you see what you are approving. A command too long to show is refused.
- Shell and AppleScript commands that name Wisp's own local service are refused, and a web page open in your browser can't make requests that change anything in Wisp. The command check reads the command's text, so it is a safety net, not a guarantee; the approval card above is the backstop.
- The cloud "Everything" option is gone from Settings. Wisp does not use a cloud model unless you set one up; with one set up, the question and recent conversation go to it (see Ask above for what never does). A setting made by hand in the config file still works, and Settings says so.

## Known issues

- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- The first Google connection sets up Wisp's Google tools, which downloads a small server from the Python package index. You need to be online, it can take a minute, and Settings > Google > Advanced shows the progress. You don't need to install anything: the tool that runs it (uv 0.12.23) is inside the app.

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem...** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Export Chat Debug Log...** in the same menu saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
