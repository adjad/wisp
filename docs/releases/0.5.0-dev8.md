**Wisp 0.5.0-dev8** is a development build for testers. It builds on [dev7](0.5.0-dev7.md), and everything those notes say still applies unless it's listed here.

## Fixes

- **Save power stays quiet, and on guard, when the engine is slow to lift a limit.** When another app starts using the graphics chip mid-reply, Wisp asks its built-in engine to lift the power limit.
  - **If the engine doesn't answer, or is too busy to:** Wisp keeps checking every second and asks again every few seconds. It no longer asks, and logs a warning, every second. It logs one warning a minute and sets no new limit during that minute.
  - **How long a limit can last:** about 2 seconds past the other app's start, plus up to about 6 seconds for each ask the engine doesn't answer.
  - **If the engine can't lift limits at all** (an older built-in engine): Wisp stops asking and sets no more limits for the rest of the run.
  - Each ask also gives up sooner. It tries at most two of Wisp's open connections, and none after one that timed out.
- **Facts from your mail are stricter about whose they are.** "My sister is moving to Denver", "I think Mike is moving to Denver" or "I met a recruiter at Google" no longer becomes a fact about you; "I just started at Stripe" and "I'm an engineer at Acme" still do. "I'm a PhD student at MIT" now counts too. A plan or a question isn't a fact yet: "I'm moving to Denver next month" or "on Friday" doesn't change your home city, and neither does "Do I live in Denver?". A word like "if" in another part of a sentence no longer drops a fact, while "I was working at" and "I wouldn't work at" now do.
- **A look-alike sender can't name a service.** A service is learned only from mail sent by its own domain, so a look-alike address (comc.io for Comcast) no longer counts, a hyphenated name like T-Mobile now does, and mail from a personal Gmail, Outlook, iCloud or internet-provider address (like name@comcast.net) never names a service.
- **Forget everything from mail keeps what you confirmed.** A fact you confirmed that came from somewhere else stays in Memory; only its link to your mail is removed.
- **Mail memory says why it's waiting, and keeps going on battery.** When mail memory pauses (you're using Wisp, other background work is running, or the app isn't open), it now records why, so a pause no longer looks like a stall. On battery it keeps reading at the 10 W background limit; only Low Power Mode or a battery under 30% pauses it.
