**Wisp 0.5.0-rc1** is a release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it now comes inside the app. This candidate is mostly about the first ten minutes: setting Wisp up and getting a first useful answer.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself.
- 16 GB of memory at least, and 24 GB recommended.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. macOS may ask you to allow permissions again; if Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

### A first run that sets you up

- **A setup window on first launch.** It picks the model for your Mac (Gemma 4 E4B), downloads it, asks for permissions, and shows you how to open Wisp. Every step is optional and can be finished later from the menu-bar icon (**Finish Setup...**).
- **Clear download feedback.** Wisp tells you before you start if you are short of disk space, shows progress, says "No progress, check your connection" if bytes stop moving, and shows whether a stopped download was paused or failed. You can keep going while it downloads, and Wisp sends a notification when it is ready. When the model has downloaded, Wisp checks that it really answers.
- **Quit and come back.** If you quit or close the window part-way, setup is still there on the next launch, at the step you left. Pressing Return on the first step downloads.
- **Permissions you understand.** The setup window explains why each one is wanted (Calendar, Reminders, Mail and Notes, Full Disk Access, Contacts, Notifications), and it is the only thing that asks while it is open. Contacts is asked for only when Wisp can read your Messages. Screen Recording and Accessibility are asked for the first time a feature needs them. **Grant More Access...** now lists only what is still off.
- **Gemma 4 E4B is the default model**, about 5.5 GB. The engine is bundled, so there is nothing else to install. Its memory guard is set so the model loads on a 24 GB Mac with other apps open.
- **Getting to know you** (what to call you, your goals, how you like replies) is offered once Wisp is ready, however long the download takes, and again later if you pick Later.
- **VoiceOver labels and announcements** in the setup window, and the window fits small displays.
- **Wisp on Macs without a notch.** No fake notch is drawn any more. The menu-bar icon shows Wisp's status, and pushing the pointer against the top-centre edge opens the panel.
- **Quit really quits.** Quitting unloads the models and stops the engine, so your memory is free again.

### Brief and Day

- **Before you ask.** Brief opens with up to five cards for things Wisp already looked into across your mail, texts, notes, calendar and reminders, each with one button to finish it: draft a reply for you to edit, set a reminder, add an event, hold time to work, or open the original. Security and account alerts come first. Tap x on any card or Needs you row and it is gone; reminders also get a check to mark them done.
- **Day** places timed to-dos on a timeline and lists the rest under "Still to fit".
- **It notices when you handled something yourself** and clears the matching card within about a minute.
- **Add-to-calendar is off by default.** Wisp no longer adds events and reminders for you from old mail and texts; the same items arrive as cards you can tap to add.

### Google (for testers on the list)

- **Connect Google** from **Settings > Google**, or from the notch's Google row before you have connected an account. Wisp reads your Gmail and Google Calendar, on your Mac, to build Brief and Day.
- **You must be on the tester list.** Ask the developer to add the Google address you will use. Others see "Access blocked".
- **Google shows an "unverified app" warning.** Choose **Continue**. Wisp asks only to read mail and calendar. Drive, Docs and the other Workspace tools stay off for an account until you switch them on and reconnect.
- **Sign-in expires every 7 days** while Wisp is in testing. Wisp sends a notification; reconnect from **Settings > Google**.
- **Disconnect & erase** revokes Wisp's access and removes that account's mail, events and to-dos from Wisp.

### Safety

- Wisp does not send an email or message, or change your calendar from a card, until you approve it.
- Under full access, Wisp asks before sending anything or running AppleScript or outbound shell commands. TODO (outbound-send confirmation): this behaviour is still being built; confirm it is in this candidate before publishing these notes.
- Shell and AppleScript commands that talk to Wisp's own local service are blocked, so a command can't trigger Wisp's send and approval actions for you, even under full access.
- The cloud "Everything" option is gone from Settings. Wisp does not send your mail, calendar or messages to a cloud model unless you set one up.

## Known issues

- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- Drive, Docs and the other Google Workspace tools need `uv` installed (`brew install uv`) and are off by default.

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem...** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Export Chat Debug Log...** in the same menu saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
