**Wisp 0.5.0-rc3** is the third release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it comes inside the app.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself (for example Ollama or LM Studio, connected in Wisp's setup).
- 16 GB of memory at least. We tuned Wisp's engine settings for a 24 GB Mac and recommend that size; on 16 GB, close other heavy apps if replies stall. Wisp offers the model download only on Macs with 16 GB or more.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. Wisp doesn't ask for permissions by itself. If Brief or Day says a source isn't allowed, open **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access**, click **Permissions…**, and click **Allow**. If Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

rc3 builds on [rc2](0.5.0-rc2.md) and [rc1](0.5.0-rc1.md). Everything they told you still applies unless it is listed below.

### The model decides how to answer every question

- **Wisp now lets the model pick the tool for every question.** Before, fixed rules answered some questions first: a reminder, a weather or email check, or "send this to…" could start a tool the moment you pressed Return. Now the model reads what you asked, then decides what to look up or do.
- **It's a little slower.** Simple questions like the weather or "any new email?" take one or two extra model steps. We estimate a few seconds more on Gemma; we haven't measured it yet. Questions that already went to the model take about as long as before.
- **Checks before anything happens.** Sending a message or email still shows its card first, and the permissions you set in Settings still apply. Some things that used to need you to type an exact ID now ask instead, because the model picks the item: cancelling a scheduled email or text (the card shows who it's to, what it says and when it was due to go), and changing or deleting a saved knowledge note. These ask even with full access.
- **You can switch back.** Add `model_only: false` to `~/.moe/config.yaml` and restart Wisp to bring the fixed rules back. `/resetwisp` removes that line again.
- **Questions about one of your things get read first.** "Explain the email from Alex" now reads that email instead of answering from general knowledge, and "define X; delete the Laundry reminder" no longer drops the second part.
- If your Ask model is a cloud model or another app on your Mac (LM Studio, Ollama), Wisp keeps the fixed rules for it, as in rc2. The same goes if you turned on **Super Model**, so your questions still go to the cloud model when it helps.
- **If an earlier action in a chat may not have finished**, Wisp now says so at the top of its answer and won't take new actions in that chat until you check it. Start a new chat to carry on.

### Setup that tells you what's going on

- **Every permission in one place.** The setup window's Permissions step now also has an optional **More access** section with **Screen Recording** and **Accessibility**, for asking Wisp about what's on your screen. Setup doesn't need them, and they don't count toward "still off". Only the microphone waits until you first use voice, and macOS's own "Wisp wants to control…" questions still appear the first time Wisp uses that app. Each row asks only when you click it. Screen Recording starts working after Wisp quits and reopens; setup picks up at the same step.
- **Contacts no longer gets stuck.** On a Mac without Messages, Contacts used to wait for Full Disk Access forever and the step read "1 still off". Now it has its own **Allow**.
- **If Wisp is slow to start, it says so.** After 3 minutes on "Starting Wisp…", setup says it's taking longer than usual and offers **Check again**.
- **Open Wisp when you log in.** The last step has an optional switch for it. It's off unless you turn it on.
- **The last step teaches voice.** It shows **⌥ Space** to ask and **⌥ ⇧ Space** to ask by voice, and says "That's it" when you press ⌥ Space. The "Try asking" questions wait until the model is ready.
- **Download wording matches what happens.** **Pause** is now **Stop**, and the text says **Resume** starts it again. The free-space warning shows on every download button, not just the first. If your Mac sleeps mid-download, setup and the notch say so when it wakes.
- **Cloud-only setups skip the download.** If your Ask model is already a cloud model, setup no longer offers a model download.
- **Clearer Google step.** It now says your account must be on the tester list, that Google warns the app isn't verified yet, and that sign-in lasts 7 days.
- **You hear when Wisp is ready, even with notifications off.** If you skipped notifications, the notch shows "Wisp is ready" the next time you open it.
- **Your first Brief starts as soon as the model is ready,** even if the download finishes after you close setup.
- **Getting to know you, once more.** If you skip it during setup, the notch offers it one more time a few days later, and never again after that.
- **One window from the Dock.** A minimized setup window no longer comes back beside the setup guide.
- **Report a Problem includes how setup went:** the steps reached and when the download started, stopped or finished. It holds no personal details.

### Ask and the Brief help you fix things

- **An Allow button when Ask can't read something.** If a question needs Calendar, Reminders, Mail, Messages, Notes or Contacts and Wisp isn't allowed, the answer now has an **Allow in Wisp…** or **Open Settings** button.
- **Suggested questions are about you.** Ask's starters can name your next event or someone waiting on a reply, greet you by name if you've told Wisp, and offer help with the first goal you gave in Getting to know you. The goal one appears only while no cloud model is set to answer Ask, since what you told Wisp there never goes to the cloud. Starters for a source Wisp can't read are hidden, in the notch and in the Wisp window.
- **The Brief says what happens and when.** Before your first summary it shows its real time instead of "arrives with your morning brief", and "Building your first Brief…" while it's on its way. The first Brief and "What can you do?" say when Wisp sends summaries, asks how your day went, and learns routines.
- **"What can you do?" mentions voice,** and only suggests connecting Google when this copy of Wisp has Google sign-in.
- **Fix links for Gmail and Contacts.** A Gmail account that needs reconnecting has a **Reconnect in Settings ▸ Google** link in the Brief, and a Contacts denial shows a row with a fix link in Brief and Day.
- **Keeping in mind** moved up in the Brief, with **Show all** when there are more than four.

### A shorter menu-bar menu

- **Settings and Setup open in the Wisp window.** **Settings…**, ⌘, and the notch's All settings open Settings inside the Wisp window instead of a separate window, and the full setup guide is **Settings ▸ Models ▸ Setup**. Settings takes the place of the chat while it's open, so the two can't sit side by side.
- Right-clicking the menu-bar icon now shows **Open Wisp Window**, **Settings…**, **Report a Problem…** and **Quit Wisp**, plus **Finish Setup…** while setup isn't finished.
- The other items moved:
  - change the model or engine in **Settings ▸ Models** (**Setup guide…** is there too);
  - **Permissions…** and **More Access…** (it was Grant More Access…) are buttons in **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access**;
  - **Export Chat Debug Log…** is in **Settings ▸ General**;
  - Getting to know you is `/about` in the notch, or **Memory ▸ About you**.

### A clearer notch and Wisp window

- **Colour means something.** The notch stays black and white, except that errors are red and things that need you (Full access on, setup set aside, flagged Day items) are amber.
- **Bigger buttons.** The notch's top-right buttons are larger and easier to hit.
- **It says when the cloud is on.** The Wisp window's header shows **Local** only once Wisp has confirmed nothing goes to the cloud, and **Local + cloud** when a cloud model is turned on. The sidebar footer and the line under the message box say the same. If Wisp can't tell, no label is shown.
- **Text size, contrast and motion reach the Wisp window.** Text size (in the notch's quick settings) now also enlarges chats in the Wisp window. Increase Contrast makes its secondary text and lines stronger, and Reduce Motion makes its panels fade instead of slide.
- **Attaching an image in the Wisp window** is now only in Debug Mode.

### Permissions that tell you what happened

- **Allow on Reminders and Calendar always does something.** Before, while Wisp was waiting for your answer about Mail and Notes, every other **Allow** button was turned off, so clicking Reminders did nothing. Now only the row with a question up says "Asking…".
- **A click that can't ask says why.** If you already said no, the click opens that page in System Settings, and the row tells you where to switch Wisp on. If macOS doesn't show its question, the row says so and tells you to quit and reopen Wisp, then click **Allow** again.
- **Mail & Notes and Messages remember your answer across launches.** A closed Mail, Notes or Messages no longer makes the row read "Not set" after Wisp restarts. A real answer from macOS always wins over the remembered one, and if macOS forgets an answer after an update, the row says so.

### The Brief stays put

- **Cards don't reshuffle.** A card stays on the Brief until you handle it, dismiss it, answer the conversation, or it goes past due. New cards are added after the ones you've seen, so the Brief no longer jumps between three, two and four cards as Wisp checks again.
- **Dismissed is dismissed.** A card you dismiss, or a Needs-you item you clear, comes back only when there is genuinely something new, like a new message from that person. Things you cleared in an earlier build stay cleared.
- **A reply card stays until you deal with it.** Opening the message no longer makes its reply card disappear; answering, dismissing, or a new message from them does. A card stays for at most the time Wisp looks back (4 days for texts, 7 for mail).
- **The Brief and Day scroll on smaller screens.** On a shorter display, like a 14-inch MacBook Pro, a long Brief or Day now scrolls inside the notch instead of being cut off at the bottom.

### Mail suggestions that keep going

- **It starts sooner.** Wisp now reads Mail's list of your emails as soon as it can, even while you're using it. Only reading each email, and asking the model about it, wait for a quiet moment. In Low Power Mode, or under 30% battery, it waits for all of it.
- **One odd email can't stop it.** An email Wisp can't make sense of (for example a time like "at 28:00") is tried again later, then set aside, and the rest of your mail carries on.
- **It says when it can't read Mail.** If mail suggestions can't read Mail, **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access ▸ Mail suggestions** says why in plain words, for example that Full Disk Access is off, and Wisp writes a warning to its log. A stall is no longer silent.

### Pictures and short clips

- **Ask about a picture.** Click the paperclip in Ask, drag a picture onto it, or paste one with ⌘V, then ask your question (or just press Return to hear what's in it). Wisp reads text in flyers, screenshots, receipts and whiteboards.
- **Turn a flyer into an event.** If the picture shows a dated event or to-do, Wisp offers to add it with the usual card. Nothing is added until you tap it, and words written in the picture can't make Wisp do anything else.
- **Short clips, up to 30 seconds.** Wisp looks at up to 8 still frames from the clip and listens to its sound. It sees sampled frames and hears the audio; it doesn't follow motion between them. Longer clips are refused with a short message.
- **Explain my screen.** Type `/screen` to show Wisp what's on your display. It only appears if you have already given Wisp Screen Recording (in **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access ▸ More Access…**); Wisp never asks for it on its own. You see the capture first and can throw it away with one tap.
- **Your pictures stay with you.** Wisp keeps no copy and they never leave your Mac. Your chat keeps only your question and a note that a picture was attached.

### Everyday fixes

- **The paperclip no longer freezes Wisp.** Choosing a file from the notch opens the file dialog in front without locking Wisp or bouncing its Dock icon, and clicking the paperclip again brings the dialog forward. Please try it in the notch (and, with Debug Mode on, in the Wisp window) and tell us if anything is off.
- **Impossible times are refused, not guessed.** A time like "28:00" or "13pm" in a message or email is ignored instead of crashing Wisp or turning into a wrong time. An email that says "Friday at 28" still keeps its day, and "Sept" is read as September.
- **Email summaries show each email once.** A summary across several accounts no longer repeats everything, and an email that is both in Gmail and in Mail appears once.
- **Starting over stops background work first.** `/resetwisp` now stops every background job (mail suggestions, the Brief's summaries and the daily-plan email) before it erases, so nothing can write its files back afterwards.

## Known issues

- Now that the model chooses the tools, a few things the fixed rules did exactly are up to the model:
  - **Reminders:** the model works out the date and time itself, so a vague time ("later", "after lunch") may be asked about or set differently than before. Check the reminder card.
  - **A reply to an old question:** if rc2 asked you something about a reminder or a message and you answer it after updating, Wisp treats your answer as a new question. Say the whole request again.
  - **Saved knowledge:** "save a knowledge note titled …" goes through the model, which copies your text into the note instead of saving it exactly as typed. Check long notes after saving.
  - **Email and message summaries** are now written up by the model instead of shown exactly as built, so they take longer.
  - **Deleting reminders:** the model finds the reminders by searching, not by an exact list it was handed. The card always shows exactly which ones will go, and nothing is deleted if that list changes before you tap.
  - **Completing or changing a reminder:** the model picks the reminder by its title and does it without a card, as before. If two reminders have similar titles, check that the right one changed; it's easy to undo.
  - **"Send my calendar to Mom" and similar:** Wisp no longer walks you through fixed follow-up questions (which app, who to). The model asks what it needs in the conversation.
  - **"What should I focus on?"** The model gathers your sources itself. A source you exclude ("not my email") is still left out, but the fixed one-read limit is gone, so it may look more than once.
  - **Web and stock answers** no longer get the special formatting the fixed rules gave them, so they may look plainer.
  - **Different models per kind of question:** if you set a different model for coding or reasoning in Settings, Ask now uses its main model for those questions too. Turn the rules back on (above) to keep using them.
- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- **Stop** on the model download cancels it; **Resume** starts the download again, and it may not pick up where it stopped.
- The Chat window's answers don't have the **Allow** button yet; ask in the notch to get it.
- With notifications off, the "Wisp is ready" note shows only once you open the notch; nothing changes on the closed notch.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- The first Google connection sets up Wisp's Google tools, which downloads a small server from the Python package index. You need to be online, it can take a minute, and Settings > Google > Advanced shows the progress.
- If you change Mail and Notes, or Messages, in System Settings while those apps are closed, the row keeps showing the previous answer until the app is open, or until you click **Allow**.
- A Calendar set to **Add Events Only** reads "Denied" and says to switch Wisp on; what it needs is **Full Access**.
- Mail suggestions don't start while the Brief or another background job is working, so on a busy Mac the first walk through your mail can take a few evenings.
- Ask by voice is in the notch only, and needs a model that can hear (Wisp's own Gemma is the one we tested).
- Text size changes the notch and chats in the Wisp window, but not Settings, Today, Memory or Research. Changing it while you are typing in the Wisp window clears what you typed.
- Mail suggestions read Mail.app's copy only. Sent folders are recognized by their English names (Sent, Sent Messages, Sent Mail, Sent Items).
- Pictures and clips work only with a model on this Mac that can see (Wisp's own Gemma is the one we tested). With a cloud or other model, Wisp says it can't read pictures.
- Wisp sees a picture at about 280 tokens' worth of detail, so very small print on a large screenshot or a dense page may be missed.
- In a clip, Wisp sees up to 8 still frames, not motion, and it may guess which words go with which frame.
- Attaching in the Wisp window's Chat is still Debug Mode only; use the notch.
- Drag, paste and `/screen` are new and haven't been tried widely; tell us if one doesn't pick up your picture.

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem…** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Settings ▸ General ▸ Export Chat Debug Log…** saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
