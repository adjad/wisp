[Download Wisp 0.5.0-rc2 (Apple silicon)](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/releases/tag/v0.5.0-rc2)

**Wisp 0.5.0-rc2** is the second release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it comes inside the app.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself (for example Ollama or LM Studio, connected in Wisp's setup).
- 16 GB of memory at least. We tuned Wisp's engine settings for a 24 GB Mac and recommend that size; on 16 GB, close other heavy apps if replies stall. Wisp offers the model download only on Macs with 16 GB or more.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. Wisp doesn't ask for permissions by itself. If Brief or Day says a source isn't allowed, right-click the menu-bar icon, choose **Permissions…**, and click **Allow**. If Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

rc2 builds on [rc1](0.5.0-rc1.md): the first-run setup, the Brief and Day, and the safety rules are the same. Everything rc1 told you still applies unless it is listed below.

### Ask by voice

- **Ask out loud.** Click the mic in Ask, or press **⌥⇧Space** from any app, and say your question. In Brief and Day the mic is in the top-right controls: it opens Ask and listens, so you can ask about your day. Wisp listens until you pause, for at most 30 seconds, and gives up if it hears nothing for about 7 seconds. Your words appear in Ask and are sent like typed text. If you had already typed something, the spoken words are added to it for you to finish and send.
- **Your Mac does the listening.** The Ask model turns your voice into text on this Mac: Gemma 4 E4B, the default, takes about a second once it is loaded. Wisp keeps no copy of the recording, and it never leaves your Mac. A model that can't hear (one without an audio encoder, or a cloud model) is refused before any audio is sent, so a made-up transcript never becomes your question.
- **The microphone permission is asked only when you first press the mic.** If you say no, Wisp tells you and links to the Microphone page in System Settings.
- **Answers are read aloud.** An answer to a spoken question is read with your Mac's own voice (System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Spoken Content). In **Settings ▸ Voice & accessibility ▸ Read answers aloud** you can change it to every answer or to never. Pressing the mic again stops the voice.

### Accessibility

- **Reduce Motion.** With Reduce Motion on in System Settings, the notch's looping animations (the breathing orb, the thinking dots, the listening pulse) stay still and the fades are dropped.
- **VoiceOver.** The notch's buttons have labels, and VoiceOver announces every answer and error as it arrives.
- **Higher contrast.** With Increase Contrast on, secondary text, divider lines and outlines in the notch are brighter.
- **Text size.** **Settings ▸ Voice & accessibility ▸ Text size** offers Default, Large and Extra large for the text in the notch (up to 30% larger). Settings and the Wisp window keep their size.

### Suggestions from your older mail

- **Dates you might forget, found in your mail.** When you aren't asking Wisp anything, it reads your mail from the last 90 days, one email at a time, and finds things you have to do by a date: a lease notice, a renewal, a bill, an appointment, something you promised in an email you sent. The Brief shows up to three under **Found in your mail**. **Add** puts one in Reminders, **Dismiss** stops it coming back, and **Open** shows the email.
- **Only the important ones.** A suggestion shows only when it's due within 30 days and it's your own promise, from a real person, about money, about something that expires or renews (a passport, licence, insurance, lease, card or trial), or an appointment, flight, court or jury date. Newsletters and sales are held back. Everything else is kept quietly in case it becomes due.
- **Which mail it reads.** Your Sent mail, and Mail's **Primary**, **Transactions** and **Updates**. Never **Promotions**, spam, trash or archived mail. With Google connected, mail Gmail files under Promotions, Social or Forums is skipped too, whenever Wisp can list those tabs (not when Google can't be reached, or those three tabs hold 2,000 or more emails in 90 days).
- **It stays on your Mac and out of your way.** Wisp reads Mail's own copy on this Mac (it needs Full Disk Access), and the model runs on this Mac. It runs only when nobody's asking anything, never loads or swaps a model, and cancels its request as soon as you ask something. Dates are worked out by Wisp, not guessed by the model, and a suggestion has to quote the email word for word.
- **Your choice.** **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access ▸ Mail suggestions**: Show important (the default), Record only, or Off. `/resetwisp` deletes everything it found.

### Smaller on disk

- Wisp uses about 2 GB less disk space. The engine keeps at most 2 GB of saved prompts (it was 4 GB) and 256 MB of saved images, and the app is about 100 MB smaller. A reply can be a little slower on the first turn after Wisp switches models.

### Starting over

- **`/resetwisp` puts Wisp back to its first launch.** Type `/resetwisp` in full in the notch and choose **Reset Wisp** on the card. Wisp disconnects Google and revokes its access, deletes its memories, chats, About you answers, caches, saved keys and settings, resets the permissions you gave it, and reopens to the setup window. The model and engine stay downloaded and selected. Wisp never removes an account from System Settings > Internet Accounts, and it never deletes anything in Mail, Calendar, Notes, Reminders or Messages; it removes only its own access and its own copies. It doesn't clear your own oMLX app's cache, or copies on a Mini or Air you set up as a node.
- **A reset that gets interrupted finishes on the next launch.** If something can't be deleted, Wisp tries at most three times and then tells you what's left.

### Setup and everyday fixes

- **Setup looks for your own oMLX for 30 seconds.** If you already run oMLX, step 1 reads "Looking for your oMLX…" and waits up to 30 seconds before it says anything is wrong, since oMLX can take a while to start or to list its models. After that it offers **Try again**, which gives the search another 30 seconds. If you reopen setup from **Permissions…** later, it waits the same way, but Wisp only starts your oMLX for you during the first run; after that, start it yourself or click **Try again**.
- **Quieter permission prompts.** Allowing Mail and Notes, or Messages, no longer puts those apps on your screen. Wisp opens one only if it isn't already running, keeps it out of sight, asks, and closes it again. An app you already had open, or started using meanwhile, stays open. Wisp remembers your answer, so the row keeps showing it instead of "Not set" once the app is closed.
- **A gentler voice by default.** Wisp now talks calmly and kindly instead of upbeat: at most one emoji, no pile-ups of exclamation marks, and long answers come back organized, with the answer first and short paragraphs, lists and bold for the key facts. In **About you** you can still choose Upbeat or Straight to the point. If you finished the questions before, Wisp moves you to Gentle only if your tone was Upbeat, because earlier versions saved Upbeat for everyone, so choose Upbeat again if you really want it. A saved Gentle or Straight to the point stays as it was.
- **Everyday questions get answers.** A question like "what makes the sky blue" is answered from what the model knows, even when Wisp isn't searching the web for that question, instead of being refused. Anything live, like weather, prices, scores or news, still says it can't be looked up right now.
- **Your first Brief starts when setup finishes.** If the model can answer when you reach the last step, Wisp starts your first Brief right away (after 5 a.m.), instead of waiting for its normal time. It doesn't wait for a permission you skipped. On a Mac whose model isn't ready by the last step, the Brief comes at its normal time.
- **Reply cards match their draft.** A reply card's quote and summary now describe the latest messages you haven't answered, not an older ask you already dealt with. A card for a text you never answered no longer says "They texted again after your last reply."

## Known issues

- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- The first Google connection sets up Wisp's Google tools, which downloads a small server from the Python package index. You need to be online, it can take a minute, and Settings > Google > Advanced shows the progress. You don't need to install anything: the tool that runs it (uv 0.12.23) is inside the app.
- Wisp remembers your Mail and Notes, and Messages, answers only while it is open. If you change one in System Settings while those apps are closed, the row may keep showing the old answer until you click **Allow** again (this doesn't prompt twice). After you quit and reopen Wisp, a row can read "Not set" while the app is closed.
- A tone you chose as Upbeat before rc2 is moved to Gentle, and you need to pick Upbeat again in About you.
- A long-email digest adds about 5 seconds to a question that opens one.
- Ask by voice is in the notch only. The Wisp window's chat and the Research mode have no mic, and Brief and Day hand your question over to Ask. Spoken words are never sent as Research.
- Voice needs a model that can hear, and Wisp's own Gemma is the one we tested. If you pick another Ask model, voice says it can't use that model rather than guessing.
- Text size changes the notch only, not Settings or the Wisp window.
- Mail suggestions read Mail.app's copy only. A Gmail account that isn't added to Mail is used only to skip its promotions. Sent folders are recognized by their English names (Sent, Sent Messages, Sent Mail, Sent Items).
- The first walk through 90 days of mail (about 1,000 emails on a typical inbox) takes a few evenings of idle time; suggestions appear as it goes.

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem...** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Export Chat Debug Log...** in the same menu saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
