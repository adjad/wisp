**Wisp 0.5.0-dev7** is a development build for testers. It builds on [dev6](0.5.0-dev6.md), and everything those notes say still applies unless it's listed here.

## What's new

### Save power without slowing anything else

- **Lighter on battery.** When your Mac is running on its battery, Wisp holds the graphics chip to 15 W while it writes a chat reply or updates your Brief. Replies start a little later (about a quarter of a second on a typical answer) and type out about 5% slower, for about a third less graphics power.
- **Background work always at 10 W.** Wisp's background work (mail memory, learning your routines, memory capture) runs the graphics chip at 10 W all the time, plugged in too. After 10 minutes away from your Mac, the Brief runs at 10 W as well.
- **Only while it's working.** A limit is on only while that reply, Brief item or background step is being written, and is gone as soon as it's done.
- **Other apps aren't slowed.** The limit applies to the whole graphics chip, so Wisp sets it only while other apps use little of it: under 7.5% of its time, about what an app redrawing its window uses. Beside a limited reply, a light app drawing 120 frames a second took at most a tenth of a millisecond longer per frame and missed no frames. Wisp checks right before each limited request and every second while one runs, and lifts the limit within about a second when another app (a game, a video, an export) starts using more, mid-reply too.
- **Your own work isn't slowed either.** Research and voice transcription never run under a limit, and a chat reply isn't limited while either one is running. The Daily Summary doesn't use the model, so it isn't affected.
- **A little later on battery, sometimes.** The first limited request after a pause takes about a third of a second longer, while Wisp takes a second, quick look to catch an app that only just started.
- **Built-in engine only.** Only Wisp's built-in engine can lift the limit mid-request and drop it the moment a request ends. If Wisp runs on your own oMLX app, nothing is limited.
- **One switch.** The notch's quick settings switch is now called **Save power**. It's on by default; turn it off and Wisp never limits power.

### Easier on the battery when you're not using Wisp

- **Calendar and Reminders read when they change.** Wisp now reads Calendar the moment it changes and after anything Wisp adds or moves, with a quiet check every 2 minutes in between (it used to read both every minute).
- **The Day view slows down when nobody's looking.** It refreshes every 30 seconds while the notch is open or you've used your Mac in the last 10 minutes, and every 5 minutes otherwise. Opening the notch always shows the latest.
- **The hourly Brief refresh waits until you're away** (10 minutes, as for power), and skips a run when nothing it reads has changed or nobody has been at your Mac since the last one.
- **Gmail downloads each email once.** Later syncs only check which emails are still unread.
- **Mail memory goes slower on battery** while you're away.
- **The orb rests.** It breathes only in the open notch, not on the collapsed bar, in Low Power Mode, while the screen sleeps, or with Reduce Motion on.

### An easier-to-read Day calendar

- **A list instead of an hour grid.** The Day tab's calendar now lists the rest of your day in time order, one row each. Every row shows its time on the left (an event's start and end, or when a reminder is due) and its full title, so events that overlap no longer squeeze into narrow columns that hide their names.
- **Now is a line.** A red line with the time separates what's over from what's next. An event that's on now says how long is left.
- **Overdue things come next.** Overdue reminders follow the now line (after anything that's on now), marked Overdue. A reminder due later is listed at the time it's due.
- **Free time is named.** A gap between events, plans and calls reads like "Free until 6:30 PM · 25 min" or "Free 2:00 PM – 6:00 PM · 4 h", so you can see where something fits.
- **What's over folds away.** Things that ended in the last couple of hours fold into one Earlier line. Click it to see them.
- **No legend to decode.** Each row says what it is (Calendar, Your plan, Suggested, Needs you), and a colored bar on its left matches. A long day shows its first 8 things and Show more.

### Wisp learns about you from your mail

- **Your job, school and city from your own emails.** While mail memory reads your old mail, Wisp also picks up lasting facts about you: where you work, where you study, the city you live in, the people you write to often, and everyday services and accounts (your internet provider, your airline). It uses them right away, so answers fit you without you having to say it.
- **Only your own words.** Facts about you come only from emails you sent, from your own text above any forwarded or quoted part. Someone else's news in a forward or a reply is never taken as yours. Services come from your sent mail or from automated mail addressed to you, and need two emails.
- **It stays on your Mac.** Facts learned from mail are never sent to a cloud model, even with a cloud model turned on.
- **Sensitive things are left out.** Health, religion, money, politics, legal matters and similar topics are never learned.
- **It never overrides you.** If an email disagrees with something you told Wisp, the email's version waits in **Memory → Review** for you.
- **You can see and remove them.** They're listed in **Memory** as "Fact · mail" with the line they came from, and each can be deleted.

## Known issues

- If Wisp is using your own oMLX app rather than its built-in engine, nothing is power-limited.
- After you unplug, it can take up to about a minute before replies are limited.
- With the notch closed and no input for 10 minutes, the Wisp window's Day view can be up to 5 minutes behind. Opening the notch refreshes it.
- There's no switch or "Forget everything from mail" button in the app yet for facts learned from mail; you can delete them one by one in **Memory**.
- A sentence about someone close to you ("My sister started at Stripe") can occasionally be learned as a fact about you. Delete it in **Memory**.
