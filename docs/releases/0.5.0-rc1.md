**Wisp 0.5.0-rc1** is the first release candidate for 0.5.0. It builds on [dev8](0.5.0-dev8.md), and everything those notes say still applies unless it's listed here. The chat model is the same untuned Gemma 4 E4B (`gemma-4-E4B-it-oQ4e-mtp`) as dev8.

## What's new

### Look at your screen, and act on pictures

- **Look at my screen.** A new eye button in Ask, beside the paperclip, looks at the window in front or at a part of the screen you drag over. ⌥⇧L does the first from anywhere. You see what Wisp captured, and which app and window it came from, before anything is sent.
- **Private windows stay private.** Password managers and windows Wisp can tell are private browsing are never captured. They're refused as the front window and left out of a dragged region, and Wisp says what it left out. Any other browser window asks you first. Wisp can't tell when a Safari window is private, so check before tapping **Look anyway**.
- **Pictures that lead to actions.** Ask Wisp to *do* something with a picture ("add this to my calendar", "save this receipt as a note", "reply to this") and it reads the picture, then uses its usual tools. What it read is treated like an email from someone else: every change, send or web lookup asks you first, even with Full access, and offers no Always allow.
- **Look needs Screen Recording.** Without it, Wisp tells you how to turn it on and never asks macOS by itself.

### Working hours and quiet hours

- **Wisp asks when you work and when to stay quiet.** Getting to know you has a new, skippable question. The defaults are 9 to 5, Monday to Friday, and quiet from 10 PM to 8 AM. If you've already finished setup, you can set them in **About you**.
- **Quiet hours silence what asks for your attention**, and nothing else. Nudges, check-ins, Brief banners and similar notices wait until quiet hours end, or drop away if they're out of date by then. Background work carries on. Your own chats, timers, due reminders and event alerts within 3 hours of the event still arrive as usual.
- **An offer to move an event outside your working hours.** The Day tab can suggest a better time for a one-off event of your own. It's limited to one a day and once per event, and it skips events it can see repeat, events with other people and personal-looking ones like dinner or the gym. Nothing moves until you send the request and confirm it. There's an off switch in **About you**.

### Better answers in Ask

- **Your Brief is part of day and week answers.** "What's coming up", "what's on Friday" and "anything I need to do this week" include dated items Wisp found in your mail, messages and notes, under **Also from your Brief**. They're shown as information, never counted as busy time.
- **Email search finds the right email.** Results put a match in the subject or sender ahead of a word buried in a long email, and every result gets a fair share of the space the model can read. Wisp says how much it searched, including when it finds nothing. Read mail is included unless you ask about unread mail.
- **Longer memory within a chat.** Earlier parts of a long chat are kept as a short summary, so Wisp can recall up to about 20 exchanges back. Recall also offers near matches, labelled as such, when nothing matches every word.
- **Casual chat gets a direct reply**, and Wisp follows the length and format you ask for. Ask it to always answer a certain way and it remembers.
- **Several things in one request.** "Add reminders for milk, eggs and bread" adds all three. Each one still asks you first wherever it did before, and Wisp never sends the same message twice in one request.
- **Replies read the conversation first.** "Draft a response to Sam", "reply to Ed's email" or "answer her text" now reads the messages or mail first, so the draft answers what was actually said. Plain writing, like "an essay on the public response to the policy", stays plain writing.
- **"Actually Saturday" stays a day plan.** After a question about your day, "actually Saturday", "no, Friday" or "I meant tomorrow" plans that day instead of being read as a new question.
- **"Yes" does what Wisp offered.** When Wisp's answer ends by offering to set a reminder or timer or make a note, a plain "yes" does it. Calendar events still show a card.
- **Wisp won't claim something it didn't do.** If an answer says something was done when nothing ran, Wisp catches it. An answer that only promises to look something up is asked once to actually do it.
- **Math is readable.** Formulas show as real symbols, such as ŷ and β₀, in answers and read-aloud. Prices and code stay as written.
- **Stock questions can answer "how did it move".** Quotes now include the previous close when the source has it, and Wisp says so when it doesn't.
- **Draft messages show their draft card** again.

### Sending

- **Emailing someone with no saved address.** Wisp asks you for the address instead of showing a card it can't act on. Reply with just the address and your draft goes through the usual card. A saved contact's address now shows on the card.
- **A failed action no longer blocks the chat when nothing happened.** If Wisp knows an action stopped before it did anything, like a reminder with a bad date, you can carry on in the same chat. If a send might have gone out, Wisp still stops and asks you to check.

### Safety and privacy

- **Skills and connected (MCP) tools always ask first.** When Ask decides to use a skill action or an MCP tool, you see a card every time, even with Full access, and the card has no Always allow. An MCP tool you've listed as trusted read-only, and that its server marks read-only, still runs without a card.
- **MCP tools whose names suggest they only read still ask**, under Full access too. List a tool as trusted read-only to skip the card.
- **Switching off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, the firewall, AirDrop or VoiceOver always asks**, even with Full access. An earlier "always allow" no longer covers it. Turning them on, and switching off dark mode, Low Power Mode or Do Not Disturb, work as before.
- **Changing Contacts always asks**, and Always allow isn't offered. Cards now say when a memory is being changed.
- **Web pages and email text are treated as outside content**, never as instructions, when they reach the model.
- **Your personal data files are readable only by your account**, from the moment they're created. That covers Wisp's databases and backups, permissions, Brief state, caches, the audit log and exports.
- **A web search no longer asks because of an empty memory search.** If a memory search found nothing, no private data was read, so nothing extra is needed. A search that did find something still asks.

### Day tab and Brief

- **Remove a suggested block**, with **Undo**, and bring back removed suggestions later. Your own events get **Delete event**.
- **Passed events stop asking for time.** A reminder to get ready for something whose date has passed, like "before your 10/30 midterm", moves to **Old reminders** with Keep and Delete. Late work stays where it is, and nothing is deleted unless you click.
- **No more repeats.** The same reminder no longer shows under both Overdue and Still to Fit, and a review block you've kept isn't suggested again.
- **Day answers list each item once.**
- **The Brief builds about four times a day**, spread across your working hours. Between builds, new mail still shows up in the mail rows. Automatic builds wait until a chat has been quiet for 3 minutes, which keeps your fans quieter. In a long chat, one can start after about 30 minutes in a short pause between messages, and it stops if you send another. The Brief's refresh button still works whenever you want it.
- **The Brief catches up when Wisp comes back** after a long time away or asleep.

### Proactive suggestions

- **Wisp checks your Sent mail first.** A suggestion to contact someone goes away if your recent sent mail shows you've already written to them. This reads headers of recent sent mail and stays on your Mac.
- **Whole titles on cards.** Titles wrap instead of being cut off mid-word.

### Reminders

- **No more "Wisp action" lines in your notes.** New reminders Wisp creates have clean notes. Wisp keeps what it needs in a private file on your Mac. Older reminders are cleaned up automatically, a few at a time. That includes completed reminders and lists you share, and the change syncs through iCloud.

### Permissions

- **Permissions open in the notch.** A permission row in the Brief, Day or Ask opens a page that says what the permission is, why Wisp wants it, where it stands and the one thing to do. **Mac permissions** in the notch's settings lists them all.
- **The notch tucks away for what it opens,** so you can reach macOS's question or System Settings, and comes back with the answer.

### Read aloud

- **Choose the read-aloud voice** in **Settings › General › Voice**, with a speed slider and a preview. Read-aloud now uses a male voice by default. To go back to the voice set in macOS, choose **Use system voice**.

### Memory

- **Voice and picture encoders load only when used.** On Wisp's built-in engine, the parts of Gemma that hear and see (about 950 MB together) stay out of memory until you press the mic, attach a picture or use Look, and leave again after 5 minutes unused.
- **The Models page** no longer shows an idle oMLX as using memory.
- **Save memory works on your own oMLX app**, for a Gemma chat model that isn't pinned. It waits while another app has used the model in the last 30 minutes or while anything is running, and never unloads any other model. The switch says so when it can't act.

### For testers

- **Export Chat Debug Log saves every chat since Wisp opened.** It's in **Settings** and in the menu-bar icon's menu. The file goes to your Downloads folder, readable only by your account, and includes tool results such as email text, so check it before sharing. Chats you delete, or clear with **Forget what Wisp learned…**, are left out. With **Debug Mode** on, it also includes the model's full input and output.

## Fixes

- **Exporting a debug log or saving a report no longer locks the notch.** The result appears in a window that doesn't block anything, and the files are written in the background.
- **Wisp reopens after Quit & Reopen.** When you allow a permission that asks Wisp to Quit & Reopen (for example Full Disk Access or Screen Recording), Wisp now comes back by itself after a few seconds. It never reopens after you quit it yourself, or after a logout, restart, shutdown or sleep.
- **"What's new in Wisp" describes this release.** Asking Wisp what's new now answers from these notes instead of dev8's.
- **An expired Google sign-in no longer hides newer mail on this Mac.** Wisp uses Mail's copy of that account once the sign-in has expired, and signing in again ends that. Other Google errors, such as a network drop, keep the Gmail copy and its stale warning.
- **Open in Gmail opens Gmail.** The Brief card's link no longer lands on Gmail's Temporary Error page.
- **Your quiet hours survive a damaged settings file,** and a damaged reminder file keeps whatever can still be read.
- **Old deleted text leaves Wisp's database.** Words an earlier version deleted from `assistant.db` stayed in the file's free space. It's rebuilt once on the next start, so they're gone.

## Known issues

- A Safari private window can't be detected by Look; it's captured only after you tap **Look anyway**.
- Using Ask's own "forget" in a chat doesn't yet remove that chat from the debug export until Wisp quits. Deleting the chat, or **Forget what Wisp learned…**, does.
- A reply you sent from a non-Google account may not clear a suggestion to contact someone.
- A second Mac on the same iCloud account doesn't share Wisp's private reminder file, so reminders it didn't create notify as plain reminders rather than as an exam or assignment.
- With an expired Google sign-in and Mail open, Wisp shows Mail's copy, so mail only Gmail had can drop out of view until you sign in again. A lasting Google error other than expiry keeps newer mail in Mail hidden until it's fixed.
- A version of Gemma 4 E4B fine-tuned for Wisp is still in development. This release ships the untuned model; the tuned one replaces it only after it matches or beats the current model on every test.
