**Wisp 0.5.1** fixes three problems the first outside tester of 0.5.0 hit on an Apple M1 Pro with 16 GB of memory. It changes nothing else. Everything in the [0.5.0 notes](0.5.0.md) still applies unless it is listed here.

## What's fixed

### Ask on M1-class Macs

- **Every Ask reply could fail with "The local AI engine ended its response unexpectedly."** The engine log said `Thread group size (512) is greater than the maximum allowed threads per threadgroup (448)`.
- **Why.** Each Mac's graphics chip has its own limit on how much work one step of the model may ask for at once. M1 and M2 chips allow less than newer ones. A speed-up step in the engine asked for more than an M1 allows, and when the chip refused, nothing caught the refusal, so the reply ended.
- **The step that is known to ask for too much** was resized by oMLX's author in oMLX 0.7.3, and Wisp 0.5.0 already ships oMLX 0.7.3. Wisp builds from 0.5.0-dev5 to 0.5.0-rc2-beta2, including rc1, have the older engine and fail this way on M1 and M2.
- **What 0.5.1 adds.** Wisp now checks every such step against the chip's real limit the first time it runs. If the chip refuses, the engine uses its slower standard path for that step and the reply carries on. If the fast drafting mode (MTP) fails, Wisp rebuilds the reply's state and finishes it with plain decoding. After a refusal of this kind, or after three failures of any kind, that model stays on plain decoding until it is reloaded. Running out of memory and a failed GPU command still end the reply, on purpose, as before. The engine log names the step that was refused.
- **Cost.** One extra check the first time each step runs at a new size, then a small lookup each time it runs.

### Setup permissions

- **Allow on Reminders, Calendar, and Mail & Notes could do nothing until Wisp was restarted.** There were three ways a click ended with no macOS question on screen:
  - Calendar and Reminders waited for macOS with no time limit, so a question hidden behind another window, or held up behind another row's question, left the row on "Asking…" until Wisp was reopened.
  - An ask that macOS returned unanswered was final.
  - Mail & Notes and Messages were asked about 1.5 seconds after Wisp opened the app. A first launch of Mail after a macOS update can take longer, and macOS then shows nothing.
- **Now** Calendar and Reminders rows are let go after 15 seconds and offer "Ask again". An unanswered ask is made once more by the same click, with Wisp brought to the front. Mail & Notes and Messages wait up to 20 seconds for the app to finish opening before asking; if it never does, the row says to open the app yourself and click Allow again. The notch's permission page says the same.
- **Mail now stays open in the background after you allow it.** Without Full Disk Access, Wisp can only read Mail while Mail is open. Before, Wisp closed the Mail it had opened to ask, so its first read found Mail closed and your mail stayed empty until you opened Mail yourself. Notes still closes. The row says so before you click.
- **Mail is read again when Wisp's service restarts,** instead of waiting up to 15 minutes.

### The engine choice after a macOS update

- **A first launch on a macOS too old for the built-in engine** (it needs macOS 26.2 or newer) saved "use another app for the model", and that choice stuck after macOS was updated, even with no oMLX on the Mac. Setup then said "Wisp is set to use another app for its model".
- **Now** that fallback is not saved when oMLX is not installed. Wisp decides again at each launch, and picks and saves the built-in engine once the Mac can run it. A choice you make in Settings is kept as before.

## What was and was not tested

- **M1 fix.** Tested on an M5 Pro, with the M1's limit of 448 simulated, and on GitHub's macOS 26 test machines, whose virtual graphics chip really does refuse sizes above 448 for one of Gemma's steps. In both, the refused step fell back and gave the same result as the standard path. **It has not run on a real M1, M2, M3 or M4 Mac.** It is not known which Wisp build the tester was running, so it is not known whether 0.5.0 already fixes their Mac.
- **Setup permissions.** Checked by Wisp's setup tests. Which of the three causes the tester hit is not known, and why macOS would return a Reminders ask unanswered is not explained: the second try is a mitigation, not a proven fix. Not yet tried on a fresh Mac with real macOS questions.
- **Engine choice.** Checked by tests that simulate a too-old macOS, then an update. Not tried on a real macOS update.

## Upgrading

- Nothing to do for most people. Your model, settings and permissions are kept.
- **If you first opened Wisp on a macOS older than 26.2 and setup still says "Wisp is set to use another app for its model",** 0.5.1 does not undo that saved choice by itself, because Wisp cannot tell it from one you made. Open Settings > Models and switch the engine to **Built-in engine**, or delete the `engine:` lines from `~/.moe/config.yaml` and reopen Wisp.

## Known issues

- All of 0.5.0's known issues still apply.
- **On an M1 or M2, the reply in which a step is first refused is slower.** Wisp re-reads the conversation so far to rebuild its state, which with a long chat can take several seconds and briefly needs about twice the memory for it. Later replies use plain decoding, which is slower than MTP, until the model is reloaded.
- **A refused step outside the reply's drafting path** (for example a KV-cache setting that is off by default) still ends the reply, now with the step named in the engine log.
- **Mail stays open** after you allow it in setup. Quit it if you prefer; Wisp then reads mail only when Mail is open, unless you give Wisp Full Disk Access.
