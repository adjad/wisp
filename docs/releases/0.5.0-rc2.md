[Download Wisp 0.5.0-rc2 (Apple silicon)](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/releases/tag/v0.5.0-rc2)

**Wisp 0.5.0-rc2** is the second release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it comes inside the app.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself (for example Ollama or LM Studio, connected in Wisp's setup).
- 16 GB of memory at least. We tuned Wisp's engine settings for a 24 GB Mac and recommend that size; on 16 GB, close other heavy apps if replies stall. Wisp offers the model download only on Macs with 16 GB or more.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. Wisp doesn't ask for permissions by itself. If Brief or Day says a source isn't allowed, right-click the menu-bar icon, choose **Permissions…**, and click **Allow**. If Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

rc2 builds on [rc1](0.5.0-rc1.md): the first-run setup, the Brief and Day, and the safety rules are the same. Everything rc1 told you still applies unless it is listed below.

### Ask by voice

- **Ask out loud.** Click the mic in Ask, or press **⌥⇧Space** from any app, and say your question. In Brief and Day the mic is in the top-right controls: it opens Ask and listens, so you can ask about your day. Wisp listens until you pause, for at most 30 seconds, and gives up if it hears nothing for about 7 seconds. Your words appear in Ask and are sent like typed text. If you had already typed something, the spoken words are added to it for you to finish and send.
- **Your Mac does the listening.** The Ask model turns your voice into text on this Mac: Gemma 4 E4B, the default, takes about a second once it is loaded. Wisp keeps no copy of the recording, and it never leaves your Mac. A model that can't hear (one without an audio encoder, or a cloud model) is refused before any audio is sent, so a made-up transcript never becomes your question.
- **The microphone permission is asked only when you first press the mic.** If you say no, Wisp tells you and links to the Microphone page in System Settings.
- **Answers are read aloud.** An answer to a spoken question is read with your Mac's own voice (System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Spoken Content). In **Settings ▸ Voice & accessibility ▸ Read answers aloud** you can change it to every answer or to never. Pressing the mic again stops the voice.

### Accessibility

- **Reduce Motion.** With Reduce Motion on in System Settings, the notch's looping animations (the breathing orb, the thinking dots, the listening pulse) stay still and the fades are dropped.
- **VoiceOver.** The notch's buttons have labels, and VoiceOver announces every answer and error as it arrives.
- **Higher contrast.** With Increase Contrast on, secondary text, divider lines and outlines in the notch are brighter.
- **Text size.** **Settings ▸ Voice & accessibility ▸ Text size** offers Default, Large and Extra large for the text in the notch (up to 30% larger). Settings and the Wisp window keep their size.

## Known issues

- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- The first Google connection sets up Wisp's Google tools, which downloads a small server from the Python package index. You need to be online, it can take a minute, and Settings > Google > Advanced shows the progress. You don't need to install anything: the tool that runs it (uv 0.12.23) is inside the app.
- The Mail and Notes, and Messages, rows can read "Not set" while those apps are closed, even after you allowed them. Allowing again doesn't prompt twice.
- A long-email digest adds about 5 seconds to a question that opens one.
- Ask by voice is in the notch only. The Wisp window's chat and the Research mode have no mic, and Brief and Day hand your question over to Ask. Spoken words are never sent as Research.
- Voice needs a model that can hear, and Wisp's own Gemma is the one we tested. If you pick another Ask model, voice says it can't use that model rather than guessing.
- Text size changes the notch only, not Settings or the Wisp window.

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem...** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Export Chat Debug Log...** in the same menu saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
