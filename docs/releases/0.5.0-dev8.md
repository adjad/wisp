**Wisp 0.5.0-dev8** is a development build for testers. It builds on [dev7](0.5.0-dev7.md), and everything those notes say still applies unless it's listed here.

## What's new

### A smoother first run

- **Move to Applications.** Opened from a download (Downloads, Desktop, Documents, a disk image, or macOS's temporary copy), Wisp asks once at launch to move itself into Applications and reopen. Only a click moves it. An existing Wisp there is replaced only after its own question and goes to the Trash. A folder that needs an administrator gets Finder windows for a drag, never a password prompt. Builds made on this Mac and preview builds are never asked. Chats, memory and settings aren't affected.
- **Something to look at while the model downloads.** Setup shows a short tour of example Brief, Day and Ask cards, each marked Example. The menu-bar icon shows a progress ring ("Downloading 45%"), and if setup is behind other windows, Wisp tells you when it's ready.
- **Your first Brief right away.** Once setup is done, your first Brief is made straight away, at any hour, instead of waiting for the next morning. If you fill in About you later the same day, it's rebuilt once with your answers.
- **Reminders show up after Quit & Reopen.** Granting Full Disk Access asks Wisp to quit and reopen; Reminders and Calendar now appear again as soon as it's back.
- **Open at login** moved to the Permissions step and to **Settings › Privacy**.
- **Ask waits for the model.** While setup is still getting the model ready, the notch, the window and Ask say so and point to what to do next, instead of failing.
- **How to use** shows the download first, and says what Wisp is for.
- **Change your setup later.** For two days after you finish setup, right-click Wisp's menu-bar icon and choose **Change Setup…**. Any time after that, open the notch's settings and choose **Setup**. Either one opens setup at Permissions, and your model stays as it is.

### The notch

- **The notch steps aside for full screen.** When a full-screen app, video or presentation is in front, the notch's black bar disappears and every click goes to the app. Hovering the notch, ⌥Space and the menu-bar icon still open Wisp, and the bar's dots and "!" show on the menu-bar icon until you leave full screen.

### Better answers in Ask

- **Tomorrow, even misspelled.** "What's up for tommorow" now reads your calendar and reminders. Before, a typo could leave Wisp saying "checking now… one moment" without checking. If Wisp promises to look and doesn't, it's asked once to actually look.
- **Plan tomorrow, not just today.** "Help me plan my day tomorrow" lists tomorrow's events, reminders, overdue items, people waiting on you, and suggestions.
- **Your own messages read as yours.** A message summary now says "You sent" for lines you wrote, instead of naming you as if someone else shared them.
- **One card per email in the Brief.** The same email in Mail and in a linked Gmail account shows once, and **Open in Mail?** opens that exact email. A security alert is never hidden by another email that reuses its ID.

## Fixes

- **A Mac that can't get a model isn't locked out.** If Wisp's model can't be set up on this Mac at all, the notch and chat open, the notch says Wisp isn't set up yet, and a question in Ask stays in the box with a note to choose a model in **Settings › Models**. A download that stopped or failed still waits, so you can try it again.
- **Unread counts read back correctly.** An email summary now says how many are unread in a plain sentence when it differs from the total. Before, "17 emails · 14 unread" could come back as "17 unread".
- **Save power stays quiet, and on guard, when the engine is slow to lift a limit.** When another app starts using the graphics chip mid-reply, Wisp asks its built-in engine to lift the power limit.
  - **If the engine doesn't answer, or is too busy to:** Wisp keeps checking every second and asks again every few seconds. It no longer asks, and logs a warning, every second. It logs one warning a minute and sets no new limit during that minute.
  - **How long a limit can last:** about 2 seconds past the other app's start, plus up to about 6 seconds for each ask the engine doesn't answer.
  - **If the engine can't lift limits at all** (an older built-in engine): Wisp stops asking and sets no more limits for the rest of the run.
  - Each ask also gives up sooner. It tries at most two of Wisp's open connections, and none after one that timed out.
- **Facts from your mail are stricter about whose they are.** "My sister is moving to Denver", "I think Mike is moving to Denver" or "I met a recruiter at Google" no longer becomes a fact about you; "I just started at Stripe" and "I'm an engineer at Acme" still do. "I'm a PhD student at MIT" now counts too. A plan or a question isn't a fact yet: "I'm moving to Denver next month" or "on Friday" doesn't change your home city, and neither does "Do I live in Denver?". A word like "if" in another part of a sentence no longer drops a fact, while "I was working at" and "I wouldn't work at" now do.
- **A look-alike sender can't name a service.** A service is learned only from mail sent by its own domain, so a look-alike address (comc.io for Comcast) no longer counts, a hyphenated name like T-Mobile now does, and mail from a personal Gmail, Outlook, iCloud or internet-provider address (like name@comcast.net) never names a service.
- **Forget everything from mail keeps what you confirmed.** A fact you confirmed that came from somewhere else stays in Memory; only its link to your mail is removed.
- **Mail memory says why it's waiting, and keeps going on battery.** When mail memory pauses (you're using Wisp, other background work is running, or the app isn't open), it now records why, so a pause no longer looks like a stall. On battery it keeps reading at the 10 W background limit; only Low Power Mode or a battery under 30% pauses it.

## Known issues

- The notch stepping aside for full screen hasn't been tried on a real notched Mac yet. It relies on macOS reporting the menu bar as hidden in a full-screen space; if it doesn't, the notch's bar simply stays as it was. If you set the menu bar to hide on your desktop, the bar stays too, so its marks aren't hidden with the menu bar.
