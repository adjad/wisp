**Wisp 0.5.0-rc3** is the third release candidate for testers. Wisp lives at the top of your screen, in the notch. Ask it things, read a Brief of what needs you today, and see the rest of your day hour by hour. The AI model runs on your Mac, and the engine that runs it comes inside the app.

## Open it for the first time (macOS will block it)

Wisp is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later blocks the first open. Allow it once:

1. Unzip the download and drag **Wisp.app** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open Wisp. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings**, choose **Privacy & Security**, and scroll all the way down.
4. Next to the message about "Wisp" being blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
5. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** again to confirm.

On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click **Wisp.app**, choose **Open**, and confirm instead. If macOS still refuses, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` in Terminal and open Wisp again.

## What you need

- A Mac with Apple silicon.
- macOS 26.2 or newer for the built-in AI engine. The app opens on macOS 14 and newer, but on an older Mac it needs another engine you run yourself (for example Ollama or LM Studio, connected in Wisp's setup).
- 16 GB of memory at least. We tuned Wisp's engine settings for a 24 GB Mac and recommend that size; on 16 GB, close other heavy apps if replies stall. Wisp offers the model download only on Macs with 16 GB or more.
- About 8 GB of free disk space. The first-run download is about 5.5 GB.
- If you are updating from an earlier build, quit the old Wisp first. Wisp doesn't ask for permissions by itself. If Brief or Day says a source isn't allowed, open **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access**, click **Permissions…**, and click **Allow**. If Full Disk Access stops working, remove Wisp from its list with the minus button and add it again.

## What's new

rc3 builds on [rc2](0.5.0-rc2.md) and [rc1](0.5.0-rc1.md). Everything they told you still applies unless it is listed below.

### A shorter menu-bar menu

- Right-clicking the menu-bar icon now shows **Open Wisp Window**, **Settings…**, **Report a Problem…** and **Quit Wisp**, plus **Finish Setup…** while setup isn't finished.
- The other items moved:
  - change the model or engine in **Settings ▸ Models** (**Setup guide…** is there too);
  - **Permissions…** and **More Access…** (it was Grant More Access…) are buttons in **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access**;
  - **Export Chat Debug Log…** is in **Settings ▸ General**;
  - Getting to know you is `/about` in the notch, or **Memory ▸ About you**.

### Permissions that tell you what happened

- **Allow on Reminders and Calendar always does something.** Before, while Wisp was waiting for your answer about Mail and Notes, every other **Allow** button was turned off, so clicking Reminders did nothing. Now only the row with a question up says "Asking…".
- **A click that can't ask says why.** If you already said no, the click opens that page in System Settings, and the row tells you where to switch Wisp on. If macOS doesn't show its question, the row says so and tells you to quit and reopen Wisp, then click **Allow** again.
- **Mail & Notes and Messages remember your answer across launches.** A closed Mail, Notes or Messages no longer makes the row read "Not set" after Wisp restarts. A real answer from macOS always wins over the remembered one, and if macOS forgets an answer after an update, the row says so.

### The Brief stays put

- **Cards don't reshuffle.** A card stays on the Brief until you handle it, dismiss it, answer the conversation, or it goes past due. New cards are added after the ones you've seen, so the Brief no longer jumps between three, two and four cards as Wisp checks again.
- **Dismissed is dismissed.** A card you dismiss, or a Needs-you item you clear, comes back only when there is genuinely something new, like a new message from that person. Things you cleared in an earlier build stay cleared.
- **A reply card stays until you deal with it.** Opening the message no longer makes its reply card disappear; answering, dismissing, or a new message from them does. A card stays for at most the time Wisp looks back (4 days for texts, 7 for mail).

### Mail suggestions that keep going

- **It starts sooner.** Wisp now reads Mail's list of your emails as soon as it can, even while you're using it. Only reading each email, and asking the model about it, wait for a quiet moment. In Low Power Mode, or under 30% battery, it waits for all of it.
- **One odd email can't stop it.** An email Wisp can't make sense of (for example a time like "at 28:00") is tried again later, then set aside, and the rest of your mail carries on.
- **It says when it can't read Mail.** If mail suggestions can't read Mail, **Settings ▸ Privacy & Access ▸ Mail suggestions** says why in plain words, for example that Full Disk Access is off, and Wisp writes a warning to its log. A stall is no longer silent.

### Everyday fixes

- **Impossible times are refused, not guessed.** A time like "28:00" or "13pm" in a message or email is ignored instead of crashing Wisp or turning into a wrong time.
- **Email summaries show each email once.** A summary across several accounts no longer repeats everything, and an email that is both in Gmail and in Mail appears once.
- **Starting over stops background work first.** `/resetwisp` now stops every background job (mail suggestions, the Brief's summaries and the daily-plan email) before it erases, so nothing can write its files back afterwards.

## Known issues

- Wisp is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so the first open needs **Open Anyway** (above).
- The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer. 16 GB Macs are tight with other heavy apps open; close them if replies stall.
- The first start of the engine can take a few minutes, and the 5.5 GB download takes as long as your connection does.
- Google sign-in works only for accounts on the developer's tester list, shows an "unverified app" warning, and expires every 7 days.
- The first Google connection sets up Wisp's Google tools, which downloads a small server from the Python package index. You need to be online, it can take a minute, and Settings > Google > Advanced shows the progress.
- If you change Mail and Notes, or Messages, in System Settings while those apps are closed, the row keeps showing the previous answer until the app is open, or until you click **Allow**.
- A Calendar set to **Add Events Only** reads "Denied" and says to switch Wisp on; what it needs is **Full Access**.
- If macOS never shows its Mail and Notes question, that row stays on "Asking…" until you close and reopen the setup window. The other rows still work.
- Mail suggestions don't start while the Brief or another background job is working, so on a busy Mac the first walk through your mail can take a few evenings.
- An email time Wisp refuses (like "Friday at 28") loses its day too, so no suggestion is made from it.
- Ask by voice is in the notch only, and needs a model that can hear (Wisp's own Gemma is the one we tested).
- Text size changes the notch only, not Settings or the Wisp window.
- Mail suggestions read Mail.app's copy only. Sent folders are recognized by their English names (Sent, Sent Messages, Sent Mail, Sent Items).

## How to report a bug

1. In the menu-bar icon's menu choose **Report a Problem…** (or type `/report` in the notch). It prepares a report under the reply it is about. Save it somewhere you can find it. It is never uploaded for you.
2. Open an issue at [github.com/adjad/wisp/issues](https://github.com/adjad/wisp/issues), or send it to whoever gave you the build. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Mac model, memory and macOS version.
3. For setup or engine trouble, also look at these logs: `$TMPDIR/moe-backend.log` (in Terminal: `open "$TMPDIR"`) and `~/.moe/engine/engine.log`. **Settings ▸ General ▸ Export Chat Debug Log…** saves the detail of the current chat.

Quit, uninstall and reset steps are in the [README](https://github.com/adjad/wisp#quit-uninstall-and-reset).
