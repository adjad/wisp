**Wisp 0.5.0-dev7** is a development build for testers. It builds on [dev6](0.5.0-dev6.md), and everything those notes say still applies unless it's listed here.

## What's new

### Save power on battery

- **Lighter on battery.** When your Mac is running on its battery, Wisp holds the graphics chip to 15 W while it writes a chat reply or updates your Brief. Replies start a little later (about a quarter of a second on a typical answer) and type out about 5% slower, for about a third less graphics power. Plugged in, nothing changes.
- **Only while it's working.** The limit is on only while that reply or Brief item is being written, and is gone as soon as it's done.
- **Background work at 10 W.** On battery, and after 10 minutes away from your Mac, Wisp's background work (mail memory, routines, memory capture) runs at 10 W. While you're away, so does the Brief.
- **Other apps never slow down.** The limit applies to the whole graphics chip, so Wisp sets it only while no other app is using it. It checks right before each limited request and every second while one runs. The moment another app starts, a game, a video or an export, the limit lifts, mid-reply too.
- **Built-in engine only.** Only Wisp's built-in engine can lift the limit mid-request and drop it the moment a request ends. If Wisp runs on your own oMLX app, nothing is limited.
- **Research and everything else you start run at full power.** The Daily Summary doesn't use the model, so it isn't affected.
- **One switch.** The notch's quick settings switch is now called **Save power**. It's on by default; turn it off and Wisp never limits power.

### Easier on the battery when you're not using Wisp

- **Calendar and Reminders read when they change.** Wisp now reads Calendar the moment it changes and after anything Wisp adds or moves, with a quiet check every 2 minutes in between (it used to read both every minute).
- **The Day view slows down when nobody's looking.** It refreshes every 30 seconds while the notch is open or you've used your Mac in the last 10 minutes, and every 5 minutes otherwise. Opening the notch always shows the latest.
- **The hourly Brief refresh waits until you're away** (10 minutes, as for power), and skips a run when nothing it reads has changed or nobody has been at your Mac since the last one.
- **Gmail downloads each email once.** Later syncs only check which emails are still unread.
- **Mail memory goes slower on battery** while you're away.
- **The orb rests.** It breathes only in the open notch, not on the collapsed bar, in Low Power Mode, while the screen sleeps, or with Reduce Motion on.

### An easier-to-read Day calendar

- **A list instead of an hour grid.** The Day tab's calendar now lists the rest of your day in time order, one row each. Every row shows its time on the left (an event's start and end, or when a reminder is due) and its full title, so events that overlap no longer squeeze into narrow columns that hide their names.
- **Now is a line.** A red line with the time separates what's over from what's next. An event that's on now says how long is left.
- **Overdue things come next.** Overdue reminders follow the now line (after anything that's on now), marked Overdue. A reminder due later is listed at the time it's due.
- **Free time is named.** A gap between events, plans and calls reads like "Free until 6:30 PM · 25 min" or "Free 2:00 PM – 6:00 PM · 4 h", so you can see where something fits.
- **What's over folds away.** Things that ended in the last couple of hours fold into one Earlier line. Click it to see them.
- **No legend to decode.** Each row says what it is (Calendar, Your plan, Suggested, Needs you), and a colored bar on its left matches. A long day shows its first 8 things and Show more.

## Known issues

- If Wisp is using a separate oMLX app rather than its own engine, a limit stays on for about 15 seconds after the reply finishes.
- After you unplug, it can take up to about a minute before replies are limited.
- With the notch closed and no input for 10 minutes, the Wisp window's Day view can be up to 5 minutes behind. Opening the notch refreshes it.
